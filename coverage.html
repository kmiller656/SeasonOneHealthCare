What's actually in Season One

A straight account of the question bank: how much of it exists, how it's organized against the PAEA/NCCPA blueprint, and where it's still thin.

Coverage, as of today

Season One is organized the way the exam is: by condition, and each condition is placed against the PAEA/NCCPA blueprint's rotations and task areas. That's the same structure that produces the seven end-of-rotation (EOR) exams and the PANCE itself. Conditions were written in blueprint-yield order, not alphabetically, so the highest-tested material exists first.

Today that coverage is deep across six rotations: Family Medicine, Emergency Medicine, Internal Medicine, Pediatrics, Women's Health, and Psychiatry. It's just getting started in Surgery. We'd rather say that plainly than round it up.

How a condition is tested

Every condition carries two questions, each testing something different. One asks you to recognize it: the same first move a clinician makes. The other assumes you already know what it is and tests what you'd actually do about it, managing it. A third layer isn't in the bundled bank yet (complications, mechanisms, the kind of twist that changes the usual answer), and we're not implying it is.

More for less

Board prep in this category is normally priced like a subscription and handed out one rationed slice at a time. Season One's approach is the opposite: build the whole bank first, then decide what, if anything, is worth charging for later. Right now that means the entire 1,900-question core, no account, no ads, works offline, for $0. That price reflects the current state of a product still in beta, and we'll say here, plainly, if that ever changes.

How this content is made

The bank is AI-drafted, written to the PAEA/NCCPA blueprint, and currently going through a review pass. It is not yet clinician-verified, and we won't claim otherwise. Automated checks confirm every question is answerable from the app's own study content and validate drug names against the National Library of Medicine's RxNorm database. Full detail is in Privacy & Terms.

Who this is for

Season One is built around you studying at 11pm the night before a rotation exam, not around a curriculum committee or a program's budget line. The questions, the offline access, and the price are all decided from that one seat: a student who needs the next question to be worth their time, tonight.
